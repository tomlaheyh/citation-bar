Recent articles only: this report covers articles added to PubMed in the last 36 months (older papers newly loaded into PubMed are left out), so long-established articles with more citations do not appear here. PubMed articles ranked by citations gained over the last 12 months, from monthly NIH iCite snapshots. This page shows the top-ranked articles; use Download full CSV to get every ranked article. Click a title to open the article in PubMed; hover over it to read it in full. The two citations columns are the article's total citation count at the start and at the end of the window; each month column is the citations it gained that month. High Trend: “Yes” when the article’s citations per day in the last six months are at least 1.5 times those in the first six (per day, because iCite snapshots are 33–45 days apart). “New” when the article was not yet in iCite at the start of the window, so its start count is 0 and a rising curve is expected.
Citation counts are NIH iCite's, taken from monthly snapshots. A month's gain is the change in an article's iCite citation count between consecutive snapshots. Pub Date is shown as PubMed gives it: a full date, or only a year and month, or only a year; dates sort as the start of their period. A negative monthly gain is an iCite correction: the article’s count went down between snapshots. An article not yet in iCite counts as 0 until it appears, and a blank count in a later snapshot carries the previous value forward.

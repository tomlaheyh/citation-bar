Organisations ranked by articles added to PubMed year to date, from author affiliations matched to the Research Organization Registry (ROR). An article counts once for each organisation named in its affiliations, so an article can count in more than one group. Papers ▲▼ compares with the same months of the prior year; "new" means the organisation had no articles then. MEDLINE and In PMC are the shares of each line's articles indexed for MEDLINE and with a copy in PubMed Central. The last table counts every article once and adds up to all articles added in the period. Click an organisation to open its ROR record.

Matching. Affiliations are matched first by hand-built rules for about 165 large institutions, then by ROR's own affiliation matcher run locally on every remaining affiliation. An organisation's ROR sub-units roll up into it (for example a university's schools and institutes). Umbrella records are not ranked themselves: governments, ministries, university systems (UC, Texas, SUNY and others), hospital networks and funders are split into their members, so each campus, agency or hospital ranks on its own. Research organisations usually ranked as one (CAS, CNRS, Max Planck, Helmholtz, Leibniz, DOE, NASA) stay whole.

Groups. The group follows the organisation's ROR type: Universities (education), Hospitals (healthcare), Government & research (government, research facilities, nonprofits, funders). Hospitals that our rules place inside a university (for example Massachusetts General Hospital in Harvard) count under that university, so the Hospitals table lists stand-alone hospitals. "Other in group" is the group's articles with none of the organisations listed above it.

All articles. Each article is counted once, in the first group it qualifies for, in the order Universities, Hospitals, Government & research. Unmatched covers articles with a ROR outside those groups (companies, or only an umbrella such as "University of London"), articles whose affiliations matched no ROR, and articles with no affiliation in PubMed. All articles means every record added in the period, of every publication type: research articles and reviews, but also errata, preprints, news, retraction notices, editorials, letters and comments, which often carry no affiliation or no author list.

MEDLINE and In PMC. MEDLINE is the record's status today; the most recent months are not fully indexed yet, so it reads low for them and is not compared with the prior year. In PMC means the PubMed record carries a PubMed Central ID. PubMed's own "free full text" filter is not part of the PubMed data (it also counts articles free on publisher sites), so In PMC is a floor for it.

Dates are PubMed create dates [crdt]. Data: a local copy of PubMed and the ROR registry.

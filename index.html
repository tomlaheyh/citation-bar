Screening at volume

Set an impact threshold, then High Impact filters the current page to results that clear it. Scan 1,000 walks up to five pages of 200 results and collects the full data set as it goes. The bar takes three to six seconds on a normal page; a full thousand takes about a minute.

Xout reopens your search with checkboxes that exclude letters, editorials, comments, errata, retraction notices, reviews, systematic reviews and meta-analyses — roughly a quarter of PubMed — with a live query preview you can copy into your methods.

Term Analysis takes up to twenty variants of one concept and returns PubMed counts for each across four field configurations, validated against MeSH, so you can see which phrasing actually finds the literature.

Where the data comes from

Every figure is traceable, because a metric you cannot attribute is a metric you cannot defend in a methods section.

  • NIH iCite — citation counts and RCR, requested in chunks of 200 PMIDs and rate-limited to iCite's published limit.
  • Europe PMC — citation counts when iCite is slow or unavailable. RCR is iCite-specific, so it shows - rather than a substituted number.
  • Semantic Scholar — influential citations.
  • Altmetric — attention scores.
  • OpenAlex — author h-index, i10-index and two-year citation rate; requires an ORCID.
  • SCImago — journal rank, bundled locally so it costs no network round trip.
  • NCBI E-utilities — article metadata.
  • Crossref — full-text links and DOI registration details.
No account. No tracking. No telemetry. Everything comes from public sources, and the extension's Data button exports every field it holds, so you can see exactly what it is working with. Privacy details →

What it deliberately doesn't do

It won't show Journal Impact Factor — that is a licensed metric, and this extension uses only sources that are free to redistribute, which is why SJR sits in that slot. Author metrics stay blank without an ORCID rather than guessing from a name match. RCR shows - when iCite is unavailable. Every gap is left visible instead of being filled with a plausible-looking substitute.

What it adds to each result

Two compact lines under every article. The first describes the paper; the second holds your export list, the authors and the journal.

  • Flags — retracted, erratum or expression of concern, preprint, MEDLINE, new and trending, plus a green row for high-impact papers by a formula published in full.
  • Article measures — citation count with a month-by-month chart, Relative Citation Ratio, influential citations and Altmetric attention.
  • Reading — the abstract inline, translated if you like, with the free full text underneath when it exists; full-text and PDF links, MeSH terms and keywords, Google Scholar and a citation graph.
  • Authors and journal — first and last authors with ORCID, institution and h-index; a journal panel with SJR quartile, volume this year, and MEDLINE and free full-text shares.
  • Search tools — Xout to drop non-primary research, prebuilt key searches, and the PubMed reports below.

Screening at volume

Set an impact threshold, then High Impact filters the current page to results that clear it. Scan 1,000 walks up to five pages of 200 results and collects the full data set as it goes. The bar takes three to six seconds on a normal page; a full thousand takes about a minute.

Xout reopens your search with checkboxes that exclude letters, editorials, comments, errata, retraction notices, reviews, systematic reviews and meta-analyses — roughly a quarter of PubMed — with a live query preview you can copy into your methods.

Term Analysis takes up to twenty variants of one concept and returns PubMed counts for each across four field configurations, validated against MeSH, so you can see which phrasing actually finds the literature.

Exporting what you find

Tick articles as you go — across pages and searches — and download them as .nbib or .ris for EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley or RefWorks, with abstracts and MeSH terms. Excel gives the page's bar data as a table, the AI pack zips up to 40 articles with free full text for an AI tool, and one click copies a PRISMA-S reference for your search. Export details →

Where the data comes from

Every figure is traceable, because a metric you cannot attribute is a metric you cannot defend in a methods section.

  • NIH iCite — citation counts and RCR, plus monthly snapshots for the citation chart, requested in chunks of 200 PMIDs and rate-limited to iCite's published limit.
  • Europe PMC — citation counts when iCite is slow or unavailable, and open access full text, shown under the abstract and used in the AI pack. RCR is iCite-specific, so it shows - rather than a substituted number.
  • Semantic Scholar — influential citations.
  • Altmetric — attention scores.
  • OpenAlex — author h-index, i10-index and two-year citation rate, keyed by ORCID. When PubMed's record has no ORCID for the first or last author, OpenAlex's record of the same article supplies it. Also RORs, authors' other retractions, journal details for the J panel, and free legal copies for the full-text link.
  • SCImago — journal rank and best quartile, bundled locally so it costs no network round trip.
  • NCBI E-utilities — article metadata, full records for the export list, and PubMed Central full text and NIH author manuscripts.
  • Crossref — full-text links and DOI registration details.
No account. No tracking. No telemetry. Everything comes from public sources, and the extension's Data button exports every field it holds, so you can see exactly what it is working with. PDFs you convert are read in your browser and never uploaded. Privacy details →

What it deliberately doesn't do

It won't show Journal Impact Factor — that is a licensed metric, and this extension uses only sources that are free to redistribute, which is why SJR sits in that slot. Author metrics stay blank when neither PubMed nor OpenAlex links the author to an ORCID, rather than guessing from a name search. RCR shows - when iCite is unavailable. Every gap is left visible instead of being filled with a plausible-looking substitute.

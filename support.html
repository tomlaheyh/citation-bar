Support

Most problems fall into one of a few categories, and several things that look like faults are the extension telling you the truth about missing data. Start here.

Ask a question or report a bug

The bar isn't showing

Work through these in order.

  1. Check you're on a supported page. The bar runs on PubMed search results, the trending page, saved collections, and individual article pages. It does not run on the PubMed home page, Advanced Search, the MeSH Database, or My NCBI.
  2. Check the toggle. Open the extension popup; the top button should read -Citation Bar On-. Click it to switch it back on, then reload the page.
  3. Reload the page. If you switched the bar on while already on a results page, it needs a reload to draw.
  4. Give it a few seconds. A normal page takes three to six seconds because the extension is fetching live data from several sources.

Blank values aren't bugs

The extension leaves a gap visible rather than filling it with a plausible-looking substitute. Here is what each gap means.

What you seeWhat it means
RCR -iCite is slow or unavailable. Citation counts fall back to Europe PMC, but RCR is an iCite-only measure, so no substitute is shown. Usually resolves on its own.
IC -Semantic Scholar has no record for this article, which is common for very recent papers.
Alt greyed outThe article has no DOI, so there is nothing to look up at Altmetric. A greyed Alt is not the same as an Altmetric score of zero.
doi greyed outNo DOI on the PubMed record. About 3% of articles.
Author metrics blankNo ORCID on the record. Author metrics come from OpenAlex keyed by ORCID; matching by name would return the wrong researcher, so nothing is shown.
MeSH emptyThe article isn't MEDLINE-indexed, so it has no MeSH terms yet. It may get them later.
SJR -The journal's ISSN isn't in the SCImago table.
On MEDLINE status in particular. A missing m flag does not always mean the article will never be indexed. A growing number of records sit in an "in-process" state for extended periods, so filtering a search by MEDLINE can quietly exclude papers that are simply waiting. The PubMed Summary report tracks this year by year if you want to see the scale of it.

Limits, and what the messages mean

Message or limitExplanation
Scan 1,000Walks up to five pages of 200 results — 1,000 articles — in about a minute.
"Scan 1,000 only works on PubMed search, trending, or collection pages"You're on an individual article page or somewhere unsupported. Run a search first.
"High Impact filter only works on PubMed pages"Same cause.
"No High Impact items found"Nothing on the page clears your threshold. Lower it in the popup — the default is 5, and it adjusts from 1 to 6.
"Limited to first 20 terms"Term Analysis takes a maximum of 20 terms. The first 20 are processed.
Special modeUp to 10 term groups separated by blank lines. Line 1 must be Special:, line 2 a period in the form Last X days/months/years, with X between 1 and 100.
DOI collectionUp to 15 DOIs can be gathered and viewed together in DOI Lookup.
"Failed to search external databases"OpenAlex or ClinicalTrials.gov didn't respond. Usually temporary.

Before you click "Reset Extension"

Read this first — the button does more than its name suggests. Reset Extension clears the extension's stored data and your browser's cache, cookies, history, local storage, IndexedDB, cache storage and service workers, for all time — not just for PubMed. Saved passwords, form data and downloads are not affected. You will be signed out of sites and your history will be gone. Chrome shows a confirmation dialog before it runs.

Try these first, in order — they fix nearly everything and cost you nothing:

  1. Reload the PubMed page.
  2. Toggle the citation bar off and on in the popup, then reload.
  3. Close and reopen the tab.
  4. If a specific data source looks wrong, wait a few minutes — iCite, Semantic Scholar and Altmetric all have occasional slow periods, and the bar recovers by itself.

Only reach for Reset Extension if the extension is genuinely stuck after all of that, and you're willing to lose your browsing history and active sign-ins.

Gathering details for a bug report

A report is far easier to act on with these attached. Both are in the extension popup.

  • The diagnostic panel at the bottom of the popup shows your Chrome version, operating system, available memory, and counts of bookmarks and installed extensions. It exists purely so you can paste it into a support message. It is read locally and never transmitted anywhere.
  • Data exports everything the extension is holding — PMIDs first, then every measure, timestamp and internal field. If the trouble is with specific articles, this is the single most useful thing to include. (Excel beside it gives just the bar's values as a tab-separated table for a spreadsheet.)

Also helpful: the exact PubMed search you ran, and what you expected instead.
